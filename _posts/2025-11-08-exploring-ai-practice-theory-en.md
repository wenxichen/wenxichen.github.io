In Volume I of *Selected Works of Mao Zedong*, *On Practice* describes the process of knowing and transforming the world through practice—both in changing nature and in changing society. A similar process of knowing and transforming is unfolding in the social and commercial transformation driven by AI.

In the process of knowing, the text describes two stages: **perceptual knowledge** and **rational knowledge**. As AI has advanced to where it is today, most of its users remain at the stage of perceptual knowledge. Knowledge at this stage depends mainly on experience—direct or indirect. In my view, on the B2C side, users' perceptual knowledge is relatively mature, especially when it comes to using AI tools for daily life, work, and programming. On the B2B side, by contrast, perceptual knowledge is still relatively underdeveloped. Many B2B users are influenced by their experience with B2C tools.

AI developers and the companies building these tools, however, have already made the transition from perceptual to rational knowledge—that is, from experience to theory. Many developers can now apply those theories to reshape their products.

"Yet with respect to the course of development, the movement of human knowledge is not complete. ... During a revolutionary period, circumstances change very rapidly; if the knowledge of revolutionaries cannot keep pace with these rapid changes, they cannot lead the revolution to victory." The same description applies to the AI revolution today. Within the ranks of this revolution there are **die-hards** whose thinking cannot advance with changing objective conditions. At the same time, there is **empty talk**—thinking that runs ahead of a given stage of the objective process, treating fantasy as truth.

Examples of die-hards and empty talk in today's AI revolution are worth illustrating.

Today, "the world has reached an era in which all of humanity consciously transforms itself and transforms the world." There are still "people who oppose transformation," but they cannot stop being transformed.

"Discover truth through practice, and verify and develop truth through practice again. From perceptual knowledge, actively develop rational knowledge; and from rational knowledge, actively guide revolutionary practice to transform both the subjective and objective worlds."
